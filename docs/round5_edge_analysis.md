# Round 5 — 50-Product Edge Identification

Generated by `tools/round5_screen.py` on 3 days of historical data
(`historical/ROUND_5/prices_round_5_day_{2,3,4}.csv`, 30,000 ticks per product,
1.5M rows total).

The structure follows the 5-phase plan: harness → per-product screen →
per-category cross-section → rank/allocate → OOS sanity.

## TL;DR (the 60-second read)

| Tier | Signal | Products | Action |
|------|--------|----------|--------|
| **1 — Premium** | Hard basket invariant | PEBBLES_{XS,S,M,L,XL} sum to 50,000 ± 2.8 | Quote-improve on legs whenever Σ ≠ 50,000. Pure arb. |
| **2 — Medium**  | Cointegrated pair | SNACKPACK_CHOCOLATE + SNACKPACK_VANILLA ≈ 19,900 (intraday σ ≈ 40) | **Quote-skew input for SNACKPACK MM (Tier 3b), NOT standalone.** Standalone taker-MR doesn't pay 4 half-spreads (~32 ticks) on a 2σ deviation (≈82 ticks). |
| **2 — Medium**  | 3-product residual | SNACKPACK_PISTACHIO/RASPBERRY/STRAWBERRY (SVD-2 std ≈ 63) | Same — fold into MM quote-skew; weaker than the pair. |
| **3 — Modest**  | Tick-level MR | ROBOT_IRONING, OXYGEN_SHAKE_EVENING_BREATH, OXYGEN_SHAKE_CHOCOLATE | Short-window AR(1) chase; per-day ac1 ∈ [-0.18, -0.07]. |
| **3 — Modest**  | Wide-spread, slow mid + basket-residual quote skew | SNACKPACK family (5 products) | Passive MM at inside quote; spread/σ ratio 2–3. Skew quotes by `−k·z` of the CHO+VAN basket residual to absorb reversion via maker fills. |
| **Skip**        | Random walk, tight spread | 30+ products in MICROCHIP/PANEL/SLEEP_POD/TRANSLATOR/UV_VISOR/GALAXY_SOUNDS, plus the volatile ROBOT_DISHES | No edge survives day-2/3/4 splits. |

**Headline:** the 10-unit position limit is a non-issue for the basket arb
because `PEBBLES_*` sum-to-50,000 is *deterministic* up to ≈3 ticks of noise.
Trade it through quote improvement on whichever leg is mispriced — taker
strategy doesn't pay (cumulative spread on 5 legs ≈ 64 ticks > max deviation
≈ 18 ticks).

The remaining 30+ products show no individual edge stronger than `~σ × 0.1`
autocorrelation; do not waste algorithmic complexity on them. Default to
passive MM at wide quotes if anything.

## Phase 1 — Diagnostic harness output

The harness (`tools/round5_screen.py`) computes a fixed schema per product:
n, mid stats, return std/skew/kurtosis, autocorrelation at lags 1/2/5/10/50,
variance ratios at k=2/5/10/50, R/S Hurst on returns, dominant FFT period
and power share, spread mean/median/std, mean book depth, trade aggregates.
Output is in `tools/round5_screen_output.json`.

A few global takeaways from the table (full sortable version below):

- **Returns are essentially white noise on the 1-tick scale for almost every product.**
  47 of 50 have |AR(1)| < 0.05; the 3 outliers are
  `ROBOT_DISHES` (-0.232, but day-4-only — see below),
  `OXYGEN_SHAKE_EVENING_BREATH` (-0.123),
  `ROBOT_IRONING` (-0.125),
  `OXYGEN_SHAKE_CHOCOLATE` (-0.089).
- **Variance ratios cluster around 1.0** for everything except those four
  products; consistent with random-walk mids at the per-product level.
- **Hurst exponents on returns** sit in [0.53, 0.58] for all products — slight
  long-range trending bias but inside R/S noise; not a signal.
- **FFT picks no dominant cycle.** The "best" FFT period is 2,000–4,300 ticks
  (1/3 to 1/8 of a day) but power share is < 0.15 in every case — wider than a
  noise floor would justify, but never a peak. No daily/intraday cycle.
- **Spread/σ ratio** is the cleanest one-shot screening metric. SNACKPACK
  family tops at 2.0–3.0 (spread is 2–3× per-tick volatility — passive MM
  candidates). Most other products sit at 0.7–1.3 (spread ≈ σ — fair quotes).

## Phase 2 — Per-product edge screen

The screen scores four hypotheses per product and picks the highest-scoring
one above its threshold:

1. **Deterministic pattern** — strong AR(1) or strong FFT peak share
2. **Mean reversion** — VR(10) << 1, VR(50) << 1, AR(1) < 0
3. **Wide stable spread (passive MM)** — spread/σ ratio
4. **Trending** — VR > 1, AR(1) > 0
5. (Else) **Skip**

**Result distribution:**
- 5 products → market_making (all SNACKPACK)
- 1 product → mean_reversion (ROBOT_DISHES; **regime-driven, see Phase 5**)
- 3 products → sub-threshold MR (OXY_CHOCOLATE, OXY_EVENING_BREATH, ROBOT_IRONING)
- 41 products → skip

Importantly, **per-product screens miss the basket structure** — every
individual PEBBLES product is a clean random walk (AR(1) ≈ 0, VR ≈ 1).
The edge only shows up cross-sectionally, which is why Phase 3 matters.

## Phase 3 — Per-category cross-sectional analysis

For each category, I computed:

- 5×5 correlation matrix of 1-tick returns
- SVD on centered mids, ranked by smallest singular values (= most stable
  linear combinations of the 5 mids)
- Best 2-product OLS fit
- Lead-lag matrix at lag-1 (max(|corr|) was 0.022 for SNACKPACK_V→P; **no
  lead-lag is tradable**, so I omit per-category lead-lag tables).

### PEBBLES — hard sum invariant (Tier 1 edge)

```
PEBBLES_XS + PEBBLES_S + PEBBLES_M + PEBBLES_L + PEBBLES_XL  =  50,000
```

Per-day verification (mids only):

| Day | mean(Σ) | std(Σ) | range |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 49,999.91 | 2.82 | [49981.5, 50016.0] |
| 3 | 49,999.97 | 2.76 | [49981.5, 50016.5] |
| 4 | 49,999.94 | 2.82 | [49981.5, 50016.0] |

**Same fair value, same noise band, every day.** The correlation matrix
is not "pebbles cluster + the basket" — instead, XS/S/M/L are pairwise
uncorrelated (0.00 ± 0.02) and PEBBLES_XL has correlation **-0.50 with
each of the other four**. That's exactly the structure of an
unconstrained 4-DOF random walk plus the constraint XL = 50,000 - (XS+S+M+L).

**Deviation distribution** (n=30,000 ticks, full 3 days):

| \|Σ-50000\| | n ticks | % |
|---:|---:|---:|
| ≥ 0.5 | 17,812 | 59.4% |
| ≥ 1.0 | 3,319 | 11.1% |
| ≥ 2.0 | 853 | 2.84% |
| ≥ 5.0 | 853 | 2.84% |
| ≥ 10.0 | 853 | 2.84% |
| ≥ 15.0 | 743 | 2.48% |

The deviation distribution is **bimodal**: nearly always within ±1 tick (rounding noise,
since mids step in 0.5), occasionally jumping to ±15 to ±18 (someone walked
the inside quote on one leg). Almost nothing in between. AR(1) of the
deviation is **0.013**; half-life is **0.2 ticks**. Mean run length when
\|Σ-50000\| > 2 is **1.0 tick**. The mispricing closes in a single tick.

**Why this matters for execution:** the cumulative half-spread across 5 legs
(median spreads 9, 12, 13, 13, 17 → half-spreads 4.5+6+6.5+6.5+8.5 = 32 per
side, **64 round-trip**) is much larger than the max deviation (18). You
**cannot taker-arb** this. The strategy is: keep a real-time estimate of
Σ; whenever Σ implies any single leg is mispriced (because the other four
have moved), aggressively join the inside on that leg. You're a maker, not
a taker. Position limit of 10/leg is essentially unlimited because
inventory unwinds the moment Σ snaps back.

Two structures:

**(a) CHOCOLATE/VANILLA pair.** Best 2-product OLS:
```
CHOCO ≈ 20,356 - 1.041 × VANILLA       resid σ = 76,  R² = 0.857
```
Per-day fair value (CHOCO+VANILLA):
- Day 2: mean 20,025, σ 42
- Day 3: mean 19,927, σ 32
- Day 4: mean 19,870, σ 48

Daily-σ inside ~50; day-to-day mean drift of ~150. The pair is *cointegrated*

**(b) PISTACHIO/RASPBERRY/STRAWBERRY triangle.** SVD rank-2 on the full

## Phase 4 — Rank and allocate

### Tier 1 — PEBBLES basket (allocate complexity here)

- **Trade**: stay short the rich leg(s), long the cheap leg(s), so the basket
  position nets to zero. With 10/leg position limit and a basket of 5 legs,
  you can hold ±50 units of basket exposure.
- **Style**: maker. Quote inside the wall on whichever leg is mispriced;
  reset quotes whenever Σ moves.
- **Naive backtest** (ignoring fees, taking at mid, ±3-tick entry, exit on
  sign change):
    - Per unit basket: pnl = +13,793 over 30k ticks (mids only)
    - With 5 ticks/round-trip cost: still +9,703 / unit basket
    - Per-day: 3,235 (d2), 3,167 (d3), 3,300 (d4) — **stable across days**.
- **Real cost**: cumulative spread 64 ticks > max deviation 18 ticks → must
  be a maker, not a taker. PnL above is an upper bound; achievable PnL
  depends on fill rate at inside-of-spread quotes.
- **Sharpe estimate**: with deviations occurring 2.8% of ticks and reverting
  in 1 tick, this is hundreds of trades/day. If we fill on 30% of mispricings
  at half the deviation, expect ~3,000–4,000 ticks/day per unit basket.
  Annualized Sharpe of the residual is enormous because the variance is
  near-zero.

### Tier 2 — SNACKPACK CHOCO/VANILLA pair (fold into Tier 3b MM as quote-skew)

**Standalone pair MR is sub-economic — confirmed by structural scan in Phase 6.**

The arithmetic:

- Pair σ ≈ 41 (intraday); 2σ entry trigger ≈ 82 ticks of deviation.
- Round-trip cost = 4 half-spreads (entry + exit, two legs each) at ~8
  ticks/leg ≈ **32 ticks**, before any fill-rate haircut.
- Net per perfectly-captured 2σ trade ≈ 50 ticks. But you rarely get exactly
  2σ → 0 captures because the residual mean-reverts past zero, and day-to-day
  fair value drifts ~50 ticks (line 314–319) leaks into rolling-z entries.
- The original ~55 trades / 30k ticks naive backtest came out small/negative
  for exactly these reasons.

**Use as a quote-skew on the Tier 3b MM template instead:**

```
residual_t = (CHO_mid + VAN_mid) − rolling_mean(CHO+VAN, 1000)
skew_CHO = skew_VAN = −k · residual_t           # both legs lean SAME direction
```

Then for each of the two MM legs:

```
quoted_bid = base_bid + skew      # rich basket → skew<0 → bid drops → buy less
quoted_ask = base_ask + skew      # rich basket → skew<0 → ask drops → sell more
```

(Cheap basket flips signs: skew>0 → bid rises, ask rises → buy more, sell less.)

This captures the reversion via *maker fills*, not taker crossings — no
incremental spread cost. No incremental position-limit budget either: the
skew biases inventory accumulation toward the side that's about to revert,
on the same SNACKPACK book the MM is already running.

`k` should be small enough that quotes never cross the book on a benign
residual; calibrate so that |skew| ≤ 1 tick at typical 1σ residual ≈ 41
(implies k ≈ 0.025). Tighten in live trading if fills are scarce.

The PISTACHIO/RASPBERRY/STRAWBERRY triangle (SVD rank-2 vector ≈ -0.65,
-0.71, -0.29) feeds the same way — apply a second skew term derived from
the 3-way residual to those three legs. Don't allocate a separate position
budget to it.

### Tier 3a — Tick-level MR (modest, do not over-engineer)

Persistent across all 3 days:

| Product | AR(1) per day (d2 / d3 / d4) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ROBOT_IRONING | -0.162 / -0.077 / -0.114 | Most stable. σ/tick 9–12. |
| OXY_EVENING_BREATH | -0.174 / -0.094 / -0.077 | Declining strength but always negative. |
| OXY_CHOCOLATE | -0.121 / -0.007 / -0.111 | Day-3 dropout = unreliable. |

Strategy template: passive MM with quote skew based on the last
1–3 returns. Position bites very fast: with σ/tick = 10 and AR(1) ≈ -0.1,
expected reversion in 1 tick is just 1 tick — barely worth crossing the
spread for.

### Tier 3b — SNACKPACK passive MM (with basket-residual quote-skew)

The SNACKPACK family has the highest spread/σ ratios in the universe (2.0–3.0
vs 0.7–1.3 elsewhere). Wide spread, slow-moving mids → quote inside the
wall on each leg, capture fraction-of-spread per fill. Inventory constrained
to ≤10/leg, but the mid moves slowly enough that you cycle inventory before
getting run over.

**Order-of-magnitude vs the standalone pair MR**: even at a conservative
1 inside-fill per 100 ticks per leg × 5 legs × 3 days ≈ 12k ticks of
captured half-spread; the pair MR at 55 trades × 30 ticks = 1.6k ticks. MM
is ~8× the pair MR on the same products *before* counting the basket-residual
quote-skew bonus.

**Quote skew (folds in the Tier 2 signal at zero extra cost):**

- Maintain `residual_t = (CHO + VAN) − rolling_mean(CHO+VAN, 1000)` (and a
  second residual from the PISTA/RASPB/STRAW triangle).
- Bias all five legs' quotes by `−k·z_residual_t` so the book leans toward
  the side that's about to revert. Exit happens passively as the residual
  closes and the inventory unwinds.
- Critical guard: never let the bias push the bid above the ask or invert
  the book. Cap |skew| at half the typical inside spread (≈ 4 ticks).

### Skip / passive MM only

The remaining 30+ products: the per-product screen flags them as random
walks with tight spreads. Pre-built passive MM at wide quotes is the only
safe template. Concretely: for each, quote at the wall (deepest visible
level on each side). You'll occasionally pick up trades when a flow goes
through.

**Do not write per-product strategies for this group.** It's not where
edge lives.

## Phase 5 — Sanity / OOS / regime checks

### PEBBLES — passes every check

- Same fair value (50,000) and same noise band (σ=2.8) on all 3 days.
- Stationary residual; no drift.
- Per-day pnl of the toy taker strategy is ~3,200/day — consistent.

### SNACKPACK CHOCO+VANILLA — fair value drifts day-to-day

- Day 2 mean 20,025 → Day 3 mean 19,927 → Day 4 mean 19,870.
- A static fair value of 19,940 (full-period mean) would be biased on
  every day. Use a **rolling** mean.
- Don't fit on day 2-3 mean and live-trade on day 4.

### ROBOT_DISHES — IS-OOS regime change, treat as TRAP

This is the one entry I'd actively warn against. Aggregate stats over 3
days are extremely tempting: AR(1) = -0.232, VR(10) = 0.46. But:

| Day | σ / tick | AR(1) | Day range |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 9.65 | -0.001 | 1322.5 |
| 3 | 10.01 | -0.004 | 1023.0 |
| 4 | **27.48** | **-0.290** | 1302.0 |

Day 4 alone drives the entire mean-reversion signal. The 3-day "edge" is a
single-day regime. A simple tick-MR strategy run on each day gives
per-trade pnl of ≈0 on days 2–3 and ≈25 ticks on day 4. **Do not assume
it persists** — if the regime resets on day 5, you'll lose money.

### Cross-strategy interaction checks

- PEBBLES legs are constrained-sum so any internal long/short combination
  is covered by the position limits independently.
- ~~SNACKPACK pair + SNACKPACK passive-MM on same product: a long/short
  pair position consumes the position limit budget for the MM template.
  Coordinate these — don't run both simultaneously on CHOCO/VANILLA.~~
  **Resolved**: pair signal is now a quote-skew on the MM template (Tier 3b),
  not a standalone strategy. Single inventory budget, no double-counting.
- No cross-category exposure to coordinate (low cross-category correlation —
  re-verified in Phase 6 across 1,225 cross-category pairs).

### Latency model

- PEBBLES: deviation half-life is 0.2 ticks. **Any latency at all destroys
  the edge.** This must be implemented as a real-time quote-improver that
  reacts within the same tick, not as a "see deviation, send taker order"
  loop.
- SNACKPACK pair MR: deviation half-life is hours. Latency-tolerant.
- Tick-level MR: AR(1) horizon is 1 tick. 1-tick lag halves the edge;
  2-tick lag kills it. Tradable only with a same-tick reaction.

### What I did NOT find (worth confirming)

- **No daily/intraday cycle.** FFT power shares are all <0.15.
- **No exploitable single-product trending.** All Hurst-on-returns in [0.53,
  0.58], variance ratios near 1, AR(1) > 0 cases all under 0.01.

### Lead-lag (UPDATE — competition hint follow-up)

The original analysis noted "no tradable lead-lag" using only lag-1 with
50-tick aggregated returns. The competition hint pushed me to scan further;
I ran a high-resolution CCF (`tools/round5_ccf.py`) on 1-tick changes at
every lag from -300 to +300 across all 50×50 product pairs. **One real,
consistent lead-lag chain exists, inside MICROCHIP:**

```
MICROCHIP_CIRCLE  →  MICROCHIP_OVAL       at lag  +50 ticks   (ρ = +0.050)
MICROCHIP_CIRCLE  →  MICROCHIP_SQUARE     at lag +100 ticks   (ρ = +0.061)
MICROCHIP_CIRCLE  →  MICROCHIP_RECTANGLE  at lag +150 ticks   (ρ = +0.061)
MICROCHIP_CIRCLE  →  MICROCHIP_TRIANGLE   at lag +200 ticks   (ρ = +0.053)
```

The CCF profile around each peak is a **single-tick spike, 5-10x its
immediate neighbors** — the signature of a deterministic exact lag, not a
slow propagation. Per-day breakdown is consistent for all four pairs (no
day flips sign; magnitudes within ~30% of the all-days estimate). The four
followers do NOT predict each other; only CIRCLE leads.

50-tick spacing across the four followers suggests a constructed structure:
each successive follower receives CIRCLE's signal 50 ticks later than the
previous. The other 9 categories show no analogous chain — the strongest
within-category lead-lag elsewhere is ρ ≈ 0.022 (noise floor).

**Tradeability**: the signal is real but small. Predicted follower move per
1σ CIRCLE move ≈ 0.6–1.3 ticks; MICROCHIP spread is 7–12 ticks. Threshold
TAKER strategies are unprofitable at every τ tested (sub-spread predicted
edge). The signal also doesn't materially help a passive maker (quote-skew
backtests show pure MM beats signal-skewed MM, +38K vs +20K with EDGE=4).

**What works**: pure passive market making on all 5 MICROCHIP products
(without using the signal as a quote bias) yields ~+47K over 3 historical
days. Implementation in `microchip_trader.py`. Per-day breakdown:
+24K / -3K / +26K — day 3 negative due to an adverse-selection regime,
days 2 and 4 compensate. The lead-lag infrastructure (CIRCLE history
buffer, β coefficients, per-tick lagged-return computation) is preserved
with `SIGNAL_GAIN=0` default — turn it on if you can find an execution
context where 1-tick predicted edge is meaningful (tighter spread, larger
size, ensemble with other signals).

## Phase 6 — Structural-relationship scan follow-up

After Phase 3 surfaced PEBBLES + SNACKPACK as the only categories with
cross-sectional structure, I ran a broader 7-test structural scan
(`tools/round5_structure.py`) to rule out (or rule in) hidden constraints
the per-category SVD might have missed:

1. **β-grid stationarity heatmap** — for every ordered pair (i,j) and
   β ∈ {-2, -1.5, -1, -0.5, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2}, min over β of
   std(P_i + β·P_j) / std(P_i) (per-day demeaned). 50×50 matrix.
2. **Full SVD per category** — all 5 singular values + their eigenvectors
   per category, not just the smallest.
3. **Rank invariants** — distribution of rank orderings of the 5 products
   in each category over time.
4. **Min/max envelope** — per-category max-min, mean, max-mean, mean-min
   time series.
5. **Ratio invariants** — std(P_i / P_j) / mean(P_i / P_j) per pair.
6. **Lagged-sum heatmap** — std of P_i(t) + P_j(t-k) for k ∈ {0,10,50,100,200}.
7. **Cross-category pair scan** — all 1,225 cross-category pairs, sum and
   diff std vs min(σ_i, σ_j).

Outputs: 8 figures in `analysis/out/round5_t{1..7}_*.png` plus
`round5_candidates.png` (time series of the top non-obvious spreads).

### Confirmations

- **PEBBLES sum invariant** restated: σ_5 / σ_1 = 0.0009, smallest eigenvector
  exactly (1,1,1,1,1)/√5. Already known; SVD signature is unmistakable.
- **SNACKPACK CHO+VAN ≈ const** restated: σ_5 / σ_1 = 0.103 (next-tightest
  category by far), smallest eigenvector dominated by CHO=-0.70, VAN=-0.68.
  Same σ ≈ 41 finding as Phase 3.
- **Strict rank anchors per category** — these are 100% of ticks, not 99%:
  - SNACKPACK: PISTACHIO always cheapest; STRAWBERRY always most expensive.
  - UV_VISOR: AMBER always cheapest.
  - PEBBLES: XS always cheapest, XL always most expensive (under the sum
    constraint, this is consistent).
  - ROBOT: IRONING is the cheapest in 96%+ of dominant orderings;
    MOPPING the most expensive.
  - SNACKPACK and ROBOT also show secondary anchors (CHOCOLATE second-cheapest
    in SNACKPACK).

  **Use as guard rails on the MM**: don't quote a product above its
  always-higher neighbor's bid, or below its always-lower neighbor's ask.
  These are not alpha — they are sanity bounds that should *never* be
  violated by a well-behaved quoter.

### Rule-outs (do not spend more time on these)

- **No third 2-product invariant.** Other than PEBBLES (5-way sum) and
  SNACKPACK (CHO+VAN pair, PISTA/RASPB/STRAW triangle), no category has a
  σ_5 / σ_1 below 0.23. The next tightest are ROBOT (0.232), UV_VISOR
  (0.258), MICROCHIP (0.276) — these eigenvectors don't have the clean
  ±0.7/±0.7 structure of a hard pair invariant.
- **No tradable cross-category linear constraint.** The top cross-category
  hits (`MICROCHIP_CIRCLE − OXYGEN_SHAKE_CHOCOLATE`,
  `PANEL_4X4 − TRANSLATOR_GRAPHITE_MIST`,
  `PANEL_1X4 + ROBOT_MOPPING`,
  `OXYGEN_SHAKE_MORNING_BREATH + PEBBLES_M`) bottom out at
  std(spread)/min(σ) ≈ 0.68. With 1,225 pairs tested that's within
  multiple-comparisons noise; the candidate-spread plots show wide bands
  (σ ≈ 263–376) that look mean-reverting only because most random-walk
  pairs do. Not worth a strategy.
- **No delayed (lagged) sum constraint within categories.** For every
  intra-category pair, lag k=0 minimizes std(P_i(t) + P_j(t−k)) at the
  tested lags {0, 10, 50, 100, 200}. The MICROCHIP CIRCLE→OVAL/SQUARE/
  RECTANGLE/TRIANGLE chain found in the lead-lag update is a directional
  *return* signal at lag 50, not a level-spread invariant — the two
  results don't conflict.
- **No multiplicative (ratio) invariant.** Low ratio CV is dominated by
  the fact that all mids cluster near 10,000, not by a structural scaling.
- **GALAXY_SOUNDS** has no linear constraint at all (σ_5 / σ_1 = 0.50 — the
  weakest of the 10).

### Net effect on the trading plan

- Tier 1 (PEBBLES) and Tier 2 (SNACKPACK pair + triangle, now folded into
  Tier 3b MM) are still the only two structural alphas worth coding.
- The strict-rank guard rails are a cheap safety check to add to whatever
  per-product MM scaffolding ships.
- Don't allocate code time to cross-category baskets — the data was scanned
  comprehensively at this resolution and nothing tradable bubbled up.
